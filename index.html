Internationally Recognised

A sanctuary protected
by international law

Savannes Bay and Scorpion Islet sit within one of the Caribbean's most formally protected coastal environments — recognised at both national and international level. This is not a marketing claim. It is enshrined in law.

01

One of two RAMSAR sites in all of Saint Lucia

Savannes Bay Marine Reserve is one of only two wetlands in Saint Lucia recognised under the RAMSAR Convention on Wetlands — a binding international treaty protecting ecosystems of irreplaceable ecological value.

02

A living ecosystem of mangroves, seagrass and coral reef

The bay encompasses an interconnected system of mangrove forest, seagrass beds, and coral reef that function as a marine nursery, carbon sink, and coastal storm shield. Seventeen fish species recorded within the mangrove wetland alone.

03

Protected in perpetuity — what that means for the bay

The PSEPA and Ramsar designations create a strong conservation framework around the bay, supporting low-impact activities while discouraging incompatible development and environmental degradation.

04

Kayak tours formally recognised in the management plan

The PSEPA Management Plan specifically recognises guided kayak tours to Scorpion Islet as an approved activity. SHER operates not in spite of this designation — but in full alignment with it.

On the Water

Conservation Code

Every SHER guest agrees to observe the following rules before entering the bay. These are not guidelines — they are conditions of the experience.

Read the full Conservation Code
01
Silence on the Water
No loud music, amplified sound, or disruptive noise while on the bay. The bay's wildlife — birds, fish, and mangrove fauna — are sensitive to sound disturbance. Quiet is part of the experience.
02
Leave No Trace
All waste returns to shore with us. Nothing — packaging, food, or incidental litter — enters the water or bay environment. Guides conduct a final check before departure.
03
No Contact with Mangroves
Kayaks and canoes maintain strict buffer distance from mangrove root systems. No physical contact with mangrove vegetation, roots, or substrate. The mangrove corridor is a marine nursery — we observe it only.
04
No Wildlife Disturbance
No feeding, touching, chasing, or deliberately disturbing wildlife. Observation is passive and at a respectful distance. Bird nesting areas, feeding zones, and fish aggregations are given full clearance.
05
No Unsanctioned Landings
Island landings are permitted only at the designated access point on Scorpion Islet, with a maximum of four guests per visit, one landing per day. No guest may enter interior vegetation or remove any natural material from the island.
06
Guide-Led Movement Only
All movement on the water follows the guide's route and instructions. Independent paddling beyond the designated area is not permitted. The guide's decision on conditions, routes, and stops is final.
